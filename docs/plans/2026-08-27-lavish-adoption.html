Plans as a review surface

Whether to adopt Lavish as the surface where you read and mark up a plan before you give the green light. This page was itself the trial: reading it, annotating it and sending the annotations back was the thing being evaluated.

Settled 2026-08-27 Codex PASS, round 1, no findings 3 of 3 decisions answered Committed and published 2026-08-27

01What is being decided

Plans are written as vault Markdown. You read them in a terminal or in Obsidian, and your reaction comes back as prose in chat. The proposal is to add one step: the plan is also rendered as a local HTML page you open in a browser, mark up element by element, and send back as targeted feedback.

Nothing is removed. The canonical loop, the vault, plan-gate and codex-review all stay exactly as they are. The only change is the shape of the surface you read the plan on, and the shape of what comes back.

What we already have, and why it falls short

Today

  • Plan is Markdown in the vault's research or specs folder.
  • You read it in a terminal pager or Obsidian.
  • Feedback arrives as prose in Telegram or the terminal.
  • I reconstruct which paragraph you meant from your description.
  • Diagrams are ASCII, or absent.

With a review surface

  • Plan is also an HTML page beside the Markdown.
  • You read it in a browser, on the desk or on a phone.
  • You select the exact sentence and attach the comment to it.
  • Feedback arrives with a CSS locator, so the target is unambiguous.
  • Diagrams are drawn, and a decision can be a control rather than a paragraph.

The 2026-08-26 decision on the loop picture is the evidence that this matters. Four rounds of Codex review of that explainer caught thirteen local factual errors and missed an invented band in the structure. You caught it by looking at the picture once. Per-claim review is good at local errors and blind to structural ones. A surface that makes the structure visible is aimed at exactly the class of error that review does not catch.

02How the tool works

Lavish is a local CLI and a local browser editor. There is no cloud service in the core loop and no account. The file stays on disk; the browser talks to a local server on port 4387.

Where it sits in our loop

It attaches at one point only: the owner gate settled on 2026-08-26. It does not become a step, and it does not touch review or implementation.

03The objection worth taking seriously

A plan that exists as Markdown in the vault and as HTML in the repo is two copies of one thing. That is the failure the deletion test is written to catch. If both are kept current by hand they drift, and the drift is silent, because each one looks correct on its own.

Three ways out, in order of how much I believe them

  • The HTML is the plan, and the vault keeps the decision, not the document. The vault already works this way for decisions: the decision log carries what was settled and why, never the working document. A plan is a working document. It can live in the repo, be reviewed, and leave behind a decision-log entry when it is settled. Nothing is duplicated because the Markdown plan stops existing.
  • The HTML is generated from the Markdown and never hand-edited. Honest, but it means writing and maintaining a converter, and the interesting part of this surface is exactly what a converter cannot produce: drawn diagrams and decision controls.
  • Accept two copies and keep them in sync by discipline. This is the one that fails. It is worth naming so it is not chosen by accident.

My recommendation is the first, and decision 3 below is where you settle it.

04What adoption costs

Every one of these is verified against the tool or the repo, not inferred from its README.

Polling discipline P3 The tool requires that poll runs in the foreground, or under a background facility that is guaranteed to wake the same agent. Claude Code's tracked background job is such a facility. Bare & or nohup is not, and must not be used.

Decision 2 · The rule for share

The P1 in the table. ht-ml.app is a third-party host and its shares are public unless a password is passed.

Answered: Never, and write it down. Now a line in the AGENTS.md provenance boundary: no artifact derived from the vault is ever published to a third-party host. lavish-axi share is never run; export writes a self-contained local file and is the only way a plan leaves the machine.

Decision 3 · Where a plan lives, and what the vault keeps

The deletion test question from section 03. This is the one that actually shapes the filing, and it is the one I would most like you to push back on.

Answered: The HTML is the plan; the vault keeps the decision. The Markdown twin is not created, so nothing can drift. This page is the plan of record for this decision, and the vault entry is what it settled. One consequence worth naming: .lavish/ is gitignored and sits outside the vault, so plans are not backed up with it.
